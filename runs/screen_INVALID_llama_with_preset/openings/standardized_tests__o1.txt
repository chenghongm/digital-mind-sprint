I take the position that universities should drop the requirement for standardized test scores in admissions.

Here are three strongest reasons for this stance:

1. **Limited predictive value**: Research has shown that standardized test scores, such as the SAT and ACT, have limited predictive value in determining a student's academic success in college. They do not account for a student's individual circumstances, such as socioeconomic status, access to quality education, or personal experiences. By dropping the requirement, universities can focus on a more comprehensive evaluation of a student's potential and abilities.

2. **Bias and inequity**: Standardized tests have been criticized for perpetuating biases and inequities in the admissions process. Students from affluent backgrounds often have greater access to test preparation resources, which can give them an unfair advantage. Additionally, students with disabilities or language barriers may face significant challenges in taking these tests. By dropping the requirement, universities can reduce the impact of these biases and create a more level playing field.

3. **Holistic evaluation**: Dropping the requirement for standardized test scores allows universities to adopt a more holistic evaluation process, considering a broader range of factors, such as academic performance, extracurricular activities, community service, and personal statements. This approach can provide a more accurate and nuanced assessment of a student's potential and fit with the university's community. By moving away from a one-size-fits-all approach, universities can attract a more diverse and talented pool of applicants.

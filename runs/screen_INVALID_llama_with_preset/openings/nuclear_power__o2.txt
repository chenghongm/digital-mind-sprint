I take position (A) Wind plus storage.

Here are my three strongest reasons:

1. **Cost and Scalability**: Wind power has become significantly cheaper over the years, with the cost of wind energy decreasing by over 70% in the last two decades. This makes it more feasible to deploy large-scale wind farms to replace coal capacity. Additionally, wind power can be deployed in multiple locations, allowing for a more distributed and flexible energy system. In contrast, nuclear power plants are typically large and capital-intensive, making it more challenging to scale up nuclear capacity quickly.

2. **Energy Storage and Grid Flexibility**: Wind power is an intermittent energy source, and integrating it into the grid requires energy storage to ensure a stable and reliable energy supply. Advances in battery technology have made energy storage more affordable and efficient, allowing for the widespread adoption of wind power. By pairing wind power with energy storage, a country can ensure a stable energy supply and take full advantage of the benefits of wind power. In contrast, nuclear power plants are typically baseload power sources, which can be inflexible and difficult to dispatch when the grid needs it.

3. **Emissions Reduction and Flexibility**: Wind power can be deployed in a variety of locations, including coastal areas, which can be used for offshore wind farms. This allows for a more flexible and diverse energy mix, reducing the country's reliance on a single energy source. Additionally, wind power can be paired with other renewable energy sources, such as solar power, to create a more resilient and adaptable energy system. In contrast, nuclear power plants are typically large and capital-intensive, making it more challenging to deploy multiple nuclear power plants in different locations.

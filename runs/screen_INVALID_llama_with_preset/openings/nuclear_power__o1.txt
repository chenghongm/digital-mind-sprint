I take position (B) Wind plus storage.

Here are my three strongest reasons:

1. **Cost and Learning Curve**: The cost of wind power has decreased significantly over the years, making it more competitive with nuclear power. Additionally, the learning curve for wind power is steep, meaning that as more wind turbines are installed, the cost per unit of electricity generated continues to decrease. This trend is expected to continue, making wind power an increasingly cost-effective option. In contrast, nuclear power has a relatively flat learning curve, and its costs are less likely to decrease significantly in the future.

2. **Scalability and Flexibility**: Wind power can be scaled up or down quickly to meet changing electricity demand, making it an ideal choice for a country looking to retire a large amount of coal capacity by 2040. Wind farms can also be built in a relatively short period, allowing for rapid deployment of new capacity. In contrast, nuclear power plants take years to build and require significant upfront investment.

3. **Integration with Renewable Energy**: Wind power can be paired with energy storage technologies, such as batteries, to provide a stable and reliable source of electricity. This allows for the integration of wind power into the grid without the need for expensive and complex grid infrastructure. In contrast, nuclear power is often seen as a baseload source of electricity, which can make it more difficult to integrate with variable renewable energy sources like wind and solar.

I argue for (B) replacing tipping with higher fixed wages.

Firstly, the current tipping system often leads to significant income inequality among restaurant workers. Servers who earn tips can make much more than those who do not, creating a disparity that can be unjust and demotivating for employees who rely on base wages alone. By increasing fixed wages, we can ensure a more equitable distribution of income across all roles within the restaurant industry.

Secondly, the inconsistency and unpredictability of tips can create financial instability for many workers. Tips vary widely based on factors such as customer behavior and economic conditions, making it difficult for servers to plan their finances or budget for expenses. A higher fixed wage would provide a more stable income, allowing workers to manage their finances more effectively and reduce stress related to income volatility.

Lastly, eliminating the tipping system could lead to improved service quality. When servers are paid a fair and consistent wage, they may be less focused on upselling or rushing through service to maximize tips. This shift could result in better customer experiences and potentially higher overall sales, as satisfied customers are more likely to return and recommend the restaurant to others.

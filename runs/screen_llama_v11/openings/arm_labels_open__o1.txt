I take the position that the two arms of a randomised trial should be labelled A and B.

Firstly, the use of letters A and B is more intuitive and easier to remember for many people, especially in a clinical setting where the focus is on the treatment being administered rather than the numerical designation of the treatment. This can lead to fewer errors and misunderstandings when communicating with patients, healthcare professionals, and other stakeholders.

Secondly, the use of letters A and B is more consistent with the way treatments are often described in medical literature and clinical practice. For example, a treatment might be referred to as "Arm A" or "Treatment B" in a research paper or clinical protocol, making it easier to follow and understand the discussion.

Thirdly, the use of letters A and B can help to reduce the perception of bias or preference for one treatment over the other. When treatments are labelled as 1 and 2, it can be perceived that one treatment is being given a numerical advantage or disadvantage, which can undermine the integrity of the trial. In contrast, the use of letters A and B presents both treatments as equal and neutral options.

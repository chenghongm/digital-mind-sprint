I take position (A) 1 and 2.

Here are my three strongest reasons:

1. **Numerical consistency**: Using numbers for arm labels (1 and 2) maintains numerical consistency throughout the trial, which can help reduce errors in data entry and analysis. This is particularly important in a two-arm trial where the primary outcome is often compared between the two groups. Using letters (A and B) may lead to confusion when performing calculations or creating summary statistics.

2. **Avoidance of alphabetical bias**: In some cases, researchers may unintentionally introduce bias when using letters for arm labels. For example, if the researcher is more familiar with the treatment arm (A), they may be more likely to enter data correctly for that arm. Using numbers (1 and 2) minimizes the risk of such biases.

3. **Easier data analysis and programming**: In many programming languages and statistical software, numbers are easier to work with than letters. Using numbers (1 and 2) can simplify data analysis and programming tasks, reducing the likelihood of errors and making it easier to automate tasks. This can be particularly important in large trials with complex analysis plans.

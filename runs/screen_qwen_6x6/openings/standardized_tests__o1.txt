I argue that universities should drop the requirement for standardized test scores in admissions (B).

1. **Diverse Measures of Potential**: Standardized tests often fail to capture a student's full potential and unique strengths. Many students excel in areas not measured by these tests, such as creativity, leadership, and practical skills. Dropping the requirement allows universities to consider a broader range of factors, including extracurricular activities, essays, and letters of recommendation.

2. **Equity and Fairness**: Standardized tests can perpetuate inequality by favoring students from more affluent backgrounds who have access to better test preparation resources. This can disproportionately disadvantage students from lower-income families or underrepresented groups. By eliminating this requirement, universities can ensure a more equitable admissions process that values all students equally.

3. **Focus on Holistic Evaluation**: Requiring standardized test scores diverts attention away from other important aspects of a student’s application. Universities can better assess a candidate's fit with their institution and program through a holistic review process that considers multiple dimensions of a student's background, experiences, and aspirations. This approach can lead to a more diverse and well-rounded student body.

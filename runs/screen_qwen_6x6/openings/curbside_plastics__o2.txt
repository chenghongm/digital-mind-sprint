I argue that the city should (B) Keep the programme.

Firstly, the city's investment in collecting and sorting mixed plastics is generating a significant return on its investment. The $600,000 recovered from resale represents a substantial financial benefit, which helps offset the $2.1 million annual cost. This means the program is already breaking even and providing a net positive economic impact.

Secondly, environmental benefits are crucial. By continuing to collect and sort mixed plastics, the city is contributing to waste reduction and recycling efforts, which can help mitigate the broader environmental impacts of plastic pollution. This aligns with global sustainability goals and can enhance the city’s reputation as an environmentally responsible entity.

Lastly, discontinuing the program could lead to increased landfill use and higher costs associated with waste management in the long term. Without the current recycling infrastructure, the city would likely face higher expenses for alternative disposal methods, potentially exceeding the current operational costs of the recycling program. Therefore, maintaining the program ensures more sustainable and cost-effective waste management practices.

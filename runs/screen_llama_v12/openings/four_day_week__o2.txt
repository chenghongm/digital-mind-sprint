I take the position that the firm should move to a four-day week at unchanged pay.

Firstly, a four-day week can lead to increased employee productivity and job satisfaction, which can result in higher revenue per employee. With an extra day off, employees may be more motivated and focused during their working hours, leading to better work quality and efficiency. This, in turn, can drive revenue growth and improve the firm's overall performance.

Secondly, a four-day week can help the firm to attract and retain top talent in a competitive job market. Many professionals value work-life balance and are willing to consider alternative work arrangements that offer more flexibility and autonomy. By adopting a four-day week, the firm can differentiate itself from competitors and attract high-quality employees who are looking for a better balance between their work and personal lives.

Thirdly, a four-day week can also lead to cost savings and reduced overheads, which can be reinvested in the business or passed on to employees in the form of higher pay or benefits. With fewer working days, the firm can reduce its energy consumption, rent, and other operational costs, which can help to improve its bottom line and increase its competitiveness. Additionally, a four-day week can also lead to reduced absenteeism and turnover rates, which can further reduce costs and improve productivity.

I take the position that universities should drop the requirement for standardized test scores in admissions.

One of the strongest reasons to drop the requirement is that standardized test scores do not accurately predict a student's potential for success in college. Research has shown that there is a significant correlation between family income and test scores, which means that students from lower-income backgrounds are often at a disadvantage when it comes to scoring well on these tests. By dropping the requirement, universities can help level the playing field and give more students from diverse backgrounds a fair chance to be considered for admission.

Another reason to drop the requirement is that standardized tests are not a reliable measure of a student's academic abilities or potential for success in college. Many students who score well on these tests may not be as well-prepared for college-level coursework, and many students who score poorly may be highly motivated and capable learners. By looking beyond test scores, universities can get a more complete picture of a student's abilities and potential.

Finally, dropping the requirement for standardized test scores can help reduce the stress and anxiety that many students experience when preparing for these tests. The pressure to perform well on these tests can be overwhelming, and it can lead to burnout and decreased motivation. By eliminating the requirement, universities can help create a more supportive and inclusive environment for students, and give them the freedom to focus on their academic and personal growth without the burden of test prep.

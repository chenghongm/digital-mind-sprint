I take the position that large cities should charge drivers a fee to enter the downtown core at peak hours.

One of the strongest reasons to implement a peak-hour fee is to reduce congestion and alleviate traffic jams. By charging drivers to enter the downtown core during peak hours, cities can discourage non-essential trips and encourage people to use alternative modes of transportation, such as public transit, walking, or cycling. This can lead to a significant decrease in traffic congestion, making it easier and faster for people to get to their destinations.

Another reason to implement a peak-hour fee is to generate revenue for the city. The fees collected can be used to fund public transportation, pedestrian and cycling infrastructure, and other initiatives that promote sustainable transportation and reduce traffic congestion. This can create a self-sustaining cycle where the fees collected are reinvested in the city's transportation infrastructure, making it more efficient and attractive to residents and visitors alike.

Finally, a peak-hour fee can also help to reduce air pollution and improve public health. By reducing the number of cars on the road, cities can decrease the amount of air pollution emitted by vehicles, which is a major contributor to poor air quality and negative health effects. Additionally, by promoting alternative modes of transportation, cities can encourage people to be more physically active, which can have numerous health benefits.

I take the position that universities should cap the proportion of A grades that can be awarded in a course.

Firstly, capping the proportion of A grades helps to maintain the value and significance of the grade. If A grades become too common, they lose their meaning and distinction, making it difficult for employers and universities to assess the quality of students' work. By limiting the number of A grades, universities can ensure that students who achieve this grade have truly demonstrated exceptional performance.

Secondly, capping the proportion of A grades promotes a more nuanced understanding of student performance. When A grades are too readily available, students may feel pressure to aim for the lowest common denominator, rather than striving for excellence. By capping the proportion of A grades, universities can encourage students to aim higher and push themselves to achieve better results.

Thirdly, capping the proportion of A grades can help to reduce the stress and pressure on students, particularly those who are already struggling. When A grades are scarce, students who are not achieving them may feel a sense of relief and accomplishment, rather than disappointment and anxiety. This can lead to a more positive and supportive learning environment, where students feel motivated to learn and grow, rather than feeling overwhelmed and stressed.

I take the position that 10% of income is better put into experiences.

One of the strongest reasons for this is that experiences tend to have a greater impact on our long-term well-being and happiness. Research has shown that the happiness boost from buying material possessions wears off quickly, whereas the positive effects of experiences can last for years. By investing in experiences, we can create lasting memories and a sense of fulfillment that can be difficult to achieve through savings alone.

Another reason is that experiences often have a greater multiplier effect on our lives. For example, traveling to a new country can broaden our perspectives, introduce us to new cultures, and create opportunities for personal growth. In contrast, saving money may lead to a more stable financial situation, but it may not have the same level of impact on our personal development.

Finally, experiences can also provide a sense of purpose and meaning in life, which is essential for a fulfilling old age. As we age, we may look back on our lives and realize that we spent too much time accumulating wealth and not enough time pursuing our passions and interests. By investing in experiences, we can create a sense of purpose and direction that can guide us through the later stages of life.

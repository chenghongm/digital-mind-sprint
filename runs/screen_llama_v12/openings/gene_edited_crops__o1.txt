I take the position that a CRISPR-edited wheat variety carrying no foreign DNA should go through the conventional-breeding pathway.

Firstly, the conventional-breeding pathway is designed for varieties that have undergone traditional breeding methods, such as cross-pollination and selection, to introduce desired traits. Since CRISPR editing is a precise and targeted form of gene editing that does not involve the introduction of foreign DNA, it can be considered a form of traditional breeding. This approach would allow the wheat variety to be evaluated based on its performance and characteristics, rather than its method of development.

Secondly, the transgenic pathway is typically reserved for crops that have been genetically modified using recombinant DNA technology, which involves the introduction of foreign DNA from another organism. Since the CRISPR-edited wheat variety does not contain any foreign DNA, it does not meet the criteria for the transgenic pathway. By not subjecting it to the transgenic pathway, we can avoid unnecessary regulatory hurdles and ensure that the variety is evaluated based on its merits, rather than its method of development.

Thirdly, the conventional-breeding pathway is generally considered to be a more transparent and accountable process, as it involves the use of traditional breeding methods that have been used for centuries. In contrast, the transgenic pathway can be seen as more complex and potentially more prone to controversy, due to the involvement of recombinant DNA technology. By using the conventional-breeding pathway, we can maintain public trust and confidence in the regulatory process, and ensure that the wheat variety is evaluated based on its performance and characteristics, rather than its method of development.

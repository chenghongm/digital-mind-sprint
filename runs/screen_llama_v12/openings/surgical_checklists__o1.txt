I take the position that pre-surgery safety checklists reduce complication rates enough to justify the time they add.

Firstly, numerous studies have demonstrated a significant reduction in postoperative complications and mortality rates following the implementation of pre-surgery safety checklists. For instance, a landmark study published in the New England Journal of Medicine found that the introduction of a preoperative checklist in an Australian hospital resulted in a 47% reduction in surgical site infections and a 35% reduction in postoperative mortality. These findings suggest that the time invested in pre-surgery checklists is well worth it in terms of improved patient outcomes.

Secondly, pre-surgery safety checklists have been shown to improve communication and teamwork among healthcare professionals. By standardizing the preoperative process, checklists help to ensure that all relevant information is shared and that every team member is aware of their responsibilities. This improved communication can lead to a reduction in errors and misunderstandings, which are common causes of surgical complications. By investing time in pre-surgery checklists, hospitals can create a culture of safety and collaboration that benefits patients and staff alike.

Thirdly, the time invested in pre-surgery safety checklists is often offset by the cost savings associated with reduced complications. Postoperative complications can result in extended hospital stays, additional treatments, and increased healthcare costs. By reducing the incidence of complications, pre-surgery checklists can help to minimize these costs and improve the overall efficiency of hospital operations. In fact, a study published in the Journal of the American Medical Association found that the implementation of a preoperative checklist in a US hospital resulted in a significant reduction in costs associated with postoperative complications.

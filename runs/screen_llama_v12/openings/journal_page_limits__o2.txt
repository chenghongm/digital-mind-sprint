I take the position that a machine learning journal should cap research articles at eight pages excluding references.

Firstly, a strict page limit helps to maintain the journal's quality and focus. By limiting the length of articles, the journal can ensure that only the most essential and impactful research is published, rather than allowing authors to pad their work with unnecessary details. This, in turn, makes it easier for readers to quickly identify and engage with the most important contributions in the field.

Secondly, a page limit encourages authors to be concise and clear in their writing. When authors know they have a limited amount of space to work with, they are more likely to carefully select the most relevant information and present it in a straightforward manner. This can lead to better-written articles that are easier to understand and more effective in communicating the author's research findings.

Thirdly, a page limit helps to make the journal more accessible to a wider audience. By keeping articles concise and to the point, the journal can make it easier for readers who may not have the time or expertise to delve into lengthy, technical papers. This can help to increase the journal's visibility and impact, as well as make it a more valuable resource for researchers and practitioners in the field.

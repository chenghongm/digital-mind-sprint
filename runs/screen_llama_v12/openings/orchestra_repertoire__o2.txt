I recommend option (A) Four familiar, six new.

Here are my three strongest reasons:

1. **Audience Engagement and Retention**: By including four familiar works, the orchestra can ensure that a significant portion of its audience will be engaged and familiar with the music. This can lead to higher attendance and retention rates, as well as increased enthusiasm and loyalty among existing patrons. In contrast, a season with too many new works may alienate some audience members who are not familiar with the music.

2. **Risk Management**: Programming six new works allows the orchestra to take calculated risks and introduce new music to its audience without overwhelming them. If some of the new works are not well-received, the orchestra can still rely on the four familiar works to provide a solid foundation for the season. This approach also gives the orchestra the opportunity to build a reputation as a forward-thinking and adventurous ensemble.

3. **Artistic Growth and Development**: By programming six new works, the orchestra can provide its musicians with opportunities to grow and develop as artists. Performing new music requires a high level of technical skill, musicality, and interpretive insight, and can help to foster a sense of camaraderie and shared artistic purpose among the musicians. This, in turn, can lead to improved performances and a more cohesive sound overall.

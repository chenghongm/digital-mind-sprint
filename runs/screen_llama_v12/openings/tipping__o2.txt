I take the position that restaurant tipping should be replaced by higher fixed wages.

One of the primary reasons for this is that tipping can create an uneven playing field for servers and bartenders. Those who receive higher tips, often due to factors outside of their control such as the type of restaurant or the customer's generosity, can earn significantly more than their colleagues. This can lead to resentment and demotivation among servers who feel they are not being fairly compensated. In contrast, a higher fixed wage would ensure that all servers are paid a living wage, regardless of their tips.

Another reason is that tipping can be unpredictable and unreliable. Servers may have good days and bad days, but their income can fluctuate wildly as a result. This can make it difficult for them to budget and plan for the future, leading to financial stress and uncertainty. A higher fixed wage would provide a stable income and allow servers to better plan for their financial needs.

Finally, replacing tipping with higher fixed wages would also help to address the issue of wage theft. Many servers and bartenders are not paid for all of the hours they work, as tips are often used to make up for the difference between their hourly wage and the minimum wage. This can result in significant losses for workers, particularly in industries where tips are low or non-existent. A higher fixed wage would eliminate the need for tips to supplement a server's income, ensuring that they are paid for all of their work.

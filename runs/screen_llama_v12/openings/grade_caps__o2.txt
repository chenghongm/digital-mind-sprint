I take the position that universities should cap the proportion of A grades that can be awarded in a course.

Firstly, capping the proportion of A grades helps to maintain the value and significance of the grade. If A grades become too common, they lose their meaning and distinction, making it difficult for employers and universities to assess the quality of students' work. By capping the proportion, universities can ensure that A grades remain a true indicator of exceptional academic achievement.

Secondly, capping the proportion of A grades promotes a more nuanced understanding of student performance. When A grades are too readily available, students may feel pressure to aim for the lowest common denominator, rather than striving for excellence. By limiting the number of A grades, universities can encourage students to push themselves to achieve higher standards, rather than simply meeting the minimum requirements.

Thirdly, capping the proportion of A grades can help to reduce the stress and anxiety associated with the grading process. When students know that only a certain proportion of grades can be A's, they are less likely to feel overwhelmed by the pressure to perform. This can lead to a more positive and supportive learning environment, where students feel motivated to learn and grow, rather than simply trying to achieve a high grade.

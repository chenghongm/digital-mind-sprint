I take position (A) Wind plus storage.

Here are my three strongest reasons:

1. **Cost and Scalability**: Wind power has become significantly cheaper over the years, with the cost of onshore wind power decreasing by over 70% in the last decade. This makes it more feasible to deploy large amounts of wind power at a lower cost. Additionally, wind power can be deployed in a modular fashion, allowing for incremental additions to the grid as the technology improves and costs decrease. In contrast, nuclear power plants are capital-intensive and require significant upfront investment, which can be a barrier to scaling up deployment.

2. **Flexibility and Integration**: Wind power can be paired with energy storage technologies, such as batteries, to provide a dispatchable source of power that can match the variability of wind output. This allows for a more flexible and reliable grid, which is essential for integrating high levels of renewable energy. In contrast, nuclear power plants are typically designed to operate at a fixed capacity factor, which can make it more challenging to integrate them into a grid with high levels of variable renewable energy.

3. **Emissions Reduction and Learning Curve**: While nuclear power does not emit greenhouse gases during operation, the production of nuclear fuel and the decommissioning of nuclear power plants do have environmental impacts. In contrast, wind power has a lower lifecycle emissions intensity than nuclear power, and the production of wind turbines has become increasingly efficient and sustainable over time. Additionally, the learning curve for wind power has been steep, with costs decreasing rapidly as the industry scales up. This suggests that wind power will continue to become more cost-competitive and emissions-intensive over time, making it a more attractive option for achieving deep emissions reductions.

I take the position that large cities should charge drivers a fee to enter the downtown core at peak hours.

One of the strongest reasons to support this position is that it can help reduce traffic congestion and alleviate the strain on the city's infrastructure during peak hours. By charging a fee, drivers are incentivized to consider alternative modes of transportation, such as public transit, walking, or biking, which can help decrease the number of vehicles on the road and ease congestion. This, in turn, can improve air quality, reduce travel times, and enhance the overall quality of life for residents and visitors.

Another reason to support a peak-hour fee is that it can generate revenue for the city to invest in transportation infrastructure and services. The fees collected can be used to fund public transportation, pedestrian and cycling infrastructure, and other initiatives that promote sustainable and efficient transportation. This can help create a more livable and sustainable city, where residents and visitors can move around easily and safely.

Finally, a peak-hour fee can help to redistribute the costs of transportation to those who use the city's infrastructure the most. Currently, the costs of transportation are often borne by all taxpayers, regardless of whether they use the city's roads and infrastructure. By charging a fee to drivers who enter the downtown core during peak hours, the city can ensure that those who benefit the most from the infrastructure are also contributing to its maintenance and improvement. This can help to create a more equitable and sustainable transportation system.

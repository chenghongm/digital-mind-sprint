I take position (A) Eight familiar, two new.

Here are my three strongest reasons:

1. **Audience Expectations and Familiarity**: A regional orchestra's primary audience is likely comprised of people who are familiar with classical music and have a certain expectation of what a concert will be like. Programming eight familiar works will cater to these expectations, making the experience more enjoyable and accessible for the audience. This approach will also help to build a loyal following and increase ticket sales.

2. **Balancing Risk and Reward**: While new works can be exciting and attract attention, they also carry a risk of alienating the audience. By programming two new works, the orchestra can take a calculated risk without overwhelming the audience. This approach allows the orchestra to introduce new music while still maintaining a strong foundation of familiar works that will appeal to the majority of the audience.

3. **Building a Strong Season Foundation**: A regional orchestra's season is a long-term investment, and programming eight familiar works provides a solid foundation for the season. This approach allows the orchestra to establish a consistent tone and style, which will help to build a loyal audience and create a sense of continuity throughout the season. The two new works can then be used to add variety and excitement to the season, rather than trying to carry the entire season on new and untested material.

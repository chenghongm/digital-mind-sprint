I take the position that the $1 million prize should be split across the team.

Firstly, the discovery was a collaborative effort made by a team of twelve individuals under the lead researcher's guidance. Each team member contributed their unique skills and expertise to achieve the breakthrough, and it's unfair to attribute the success solely to the lead researcher. By splitting the prize, the team's collective effort is recognized and rewarded.

Secondly, the lead researcher's role is not solely responsible for the discovery. While they may have provided guidance and direction, the team members were the ones who conducted the experiments, collected the data, and analyzed the results. The prize should reflect the contributions of all team members, not just the lead researcher.

Thirdly, splitting the prize promotes a culture of teamwork and collaboration within the scientific community. When team members are incentivized to work together towards a common goal, they are more likely to share knowledge, expertise, and resources, leading to more innovative and groundbreaking discoveries. By rewarding the team as a whole, the prize encourages this collaborative spirit and fosters a more productive and successful research environment.

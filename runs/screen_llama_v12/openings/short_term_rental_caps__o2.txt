I take the position that cities should cap the number of short-term rental listings allowed in residential neighborhoods.

Here are three strongest reasons for this stance:

1. **Preservation of Affordable Housing**: By capping short-term rental listings, cities can help preserve affordable housing options for long-term residents. When a large number of homes are converted into short-term rentals, it can lead to a shortage of available housing for people who need to live in the area, such as low-income families, students, and essential workers. Capping short-term rentals ensures that a certain percentage of homes remain available for long-term renters, helping to maintain a balanced and diverse community.

2. **Reducing Neighborhood Disruption**: Short-term rentals can disrupt the character and quality of life in residential neighborhoods. With a cap on short-term rentals, cities can prevent the proliferation of noisy, party-oriented rentals that can negatively impact the lives of nearby residents. This can also help maintain the aesthetic appeal of neighborhoods, which is often a key factor in property values and community pride.

3. **Enhancing Local Tax Revenue**: By capping short-term rentals, cities can ensure that a larger share of the revenue generated by these rentals stays within the local community. When short-term rentals are allowed to proliferate without regulation, a significant portion of the revenue often goes to online booking platforms and property management companies, rather than to local businesses and residents. By capping short-term rentals, cities can create a more equitable distribution of revenue, which can be used to fund local services and infrastructure.

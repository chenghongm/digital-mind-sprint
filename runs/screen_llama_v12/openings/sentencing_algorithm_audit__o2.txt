I take the position that risk-assessment algorithms used in criminal sentencing should be required to be publicly auditable, including their training data.

Here are my three strongest reasons:

1. **Transparency and Accountability**: Public audibility of risk-assessment algorithms and their training data would promote transparency and accountability in the criminal justice system. By making the algorithms and data publicly available, the public can scrutinize their accuracy, fairness, and potential biases. This would help to prevent the misuse of algorithms that may perpetuate systemic injustices or discriminate against certain groups. Regulators and the public can then hold the developers and users of these algorithms accountable for any errors or biases.

2. **Improved Algorithmic Accuracy**: Public audibility would allow experts from various fields, including data science, sociology, and law, to review and critique the algorithms and their training data. This would lead to a more robust and accurate assessment of the algorithms' performance, as well as identify potential flaws or biases. By incorporating diverse perspectives and expertise, the algorithms can be improved to better serve the public interest and ensure that they are fair and just.

3. **Prevention of Algorithmic Capture**: Public audibility would prevent the "black box" phenomenon, where algorithms are developed and deployed without transparency or accountability. This would prevent the capture of the algorithmic decision-making process by special interests or those who may benefit from biased outcomes. By making the algorithms and data publicly available, the public can ensure that the decision-making process is fair, unbiased, and serves the public interest, rather than the interests of a select few.

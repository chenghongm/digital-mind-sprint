I take the position that restaurant tipping should be replaced by higher fixed wages.

One of the primary reasons for this is that tipping can create an uneven playing field for servers, bartenders, and other service staff. Those who receive larger tips, often due to factors outside of their control such as the size of the party or the generosity of the customers, can earn significantly more than their colleagues who receive smaller tips. This can lead to resentment and inequality among staff members, making it difficult for restaurants to maintain a cohesive and harmonious work environment. In contrast, a fixed wage system would ensure that all staff members are compensated fairly and equally for their work.

Another reason to replace tipping with higher fixed wages is that it would provide greater job security and stability for service staff. Tipping is often unpredictable and can vary greatly from one shift to another, making it difficult for staff members to budget and plan for their finances. A fixed wage system would provide a steady income, allowing staff members to better plan for their financial needs and make long-term decisions about their careers and personal lives.

Finally, replacing tipping with higher fixed wages would also help to address the issue of wage theft, which is a significant problem in the service industry. Many servers and bartenders are not paid the minimum wage, as their tips are supposed to make up the difference. However, this can lead to situations where staff members are not paid for all of their hours worked, or are not paid at all if they do not receive sufficient tips. A fixed wage system would eliminate the need for tips to make up for low wages, ensuring that staff members are always paid a fair and living wage for their work.

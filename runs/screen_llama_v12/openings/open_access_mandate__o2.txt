I take the position that publicly funded research should be required to publish in open-access venues.

Firstly, publicly funded research is, by definition, conducted using taxpayer dollars. As such, the results of this research should be accessible to the public who funded it, rather than being locked behind paywalls that only academics or institutions with access to expensive journals can afford. Requiring open access ensures that the public gets a return on their investment.

Secondly, open-access publishing promotes the dissemination of knowledge and facilitates collaboration among researchers. When research is locked behind paywalls, it can be difficult for researchers in other institutions or countries to access and build upon the work. This can lead to duplication of effort, wasted resources, and a slower pace of scientific progress. By making research openly accessible, we can accelerate the advancement of knowledge and improve the overall quality of research.

Thirdly, requiring open-access publishing can help to reduce the influence of commercial publishers on the scientific process. Many commercial publishers charge exorbitant fees for publishing research, and these fees can create conflicts of interest that can skew the results of research. By requiring open-access publishing, we can reduce the power of commercial publishers and promote a more transparent and unbiased scientific process.

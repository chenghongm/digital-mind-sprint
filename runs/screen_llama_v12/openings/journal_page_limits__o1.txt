I take position (A) - a machine learning journal should cap research articles at eight pages excluding references.

Here are my three strongest reasons:

1. **Improved focus and concision**: By limiting the article length, authors are forced to prioritize their research findings and present them in a clear, concise manner. This encourages authors to focus on the most important aspects of their work, making it easier for readers to understand and appreciate the research. A shorter article length also helps to prevent authors from including unnecessary details or tangential information that may dilute the impact of their research.

2. **Enhanced reader experience**: A shorter article length makes it more likely that readers will actually read and engage with the research. Long articles can be daunting, especially for readers who are not experts in the field. By capping article length, the journal can ensure that readers can quickly and easily understand the research, even if they don't have a deep background in machine learning.

3. **Better alignment with modern research trends**: In recent years, there has been a growing trend towards more concise and focused research publications. Many top-tier conferences and journals have adopted shorter article lengths, and researchers are increasingly expected to present their work in a clear and concise manner. By capping article length, the journal can stay in line with these trends and provide a more modern and relevant platform for machine learning research.

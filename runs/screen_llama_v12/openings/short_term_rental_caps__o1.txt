I take the position that cities should cap the number of short-term rental listings allowed in residential neighborhoods.

Here are three strongest reasons for this stance:

1. **Preservation of Affordable Housing**: By capping short-term rental listings, cities can help preserve affordable housing options for long-term residents. When a large number of properties are converted into short-term rentals, it can lead to a shortage of available housing for people who need to live in the area, such as low-income families, students, and essential workers. Capping short-term rentals ensures that a certain percentage of housing remains available for long-term occupancy.

2. **Reducing Neighborhood Disruption**: Short-term rentals can disrupt the character of residential neighborhoods, leading to increased noise, traffic, and strain on local resources. Capping short-term rentals helps maintain a balance between tourism and residential living, ensuring that neighborhoods remain livable for their long-term residents. This is particularly important in areas with high concentrations of short-term rentals, where the character of the neighborhood can be significantly altered.

3. **Enhancing Tax Revenue and Community Benefits**: By capping short-term rentals, cities can ensure that a larger share of tax revenue generated from these rentals stays within the community. This can lead to increased funding for local services, such as public safety, transportation, and community programs. Additionally, capping short-term rentals can help prevent the displacement of long-term residents and small businesses, which are essential to the social fabric and economic vitality of neighborhoods.

I take position (A) Spend it on other reliability work.

Here are my three strongest reasons:

1. **Production incidents are often caused by complex interactions**: While high test coverage can catch many bugs, it may not be enough to guarantee that the system behaves correctly in all possible scenarios. Complex interactions between different components or edge cases can still lead to production incidents, even with high test coverage. By focusing on other reliability work, such as code reviews, static analysis, and monitoring, the team can identify and mitigate these complex issues.

2. **High test coverage can create a false sense of security**: Achieving 95% test coverage may give the team a false sense of security, leading them to overlook other important aspects of reliability, such as performance, security, and scalability. By prioritizing other reliability work, the team can ensure that the system is robust and reliable in all aspects, not just in terms of test coverage.

3. **Other reliability work can have a higher ROI**: Investing in other reliability work, such as code reviews, static analysis, and monitoring, can have a higher return on investment (ROI) than simply increasing test coverage. These activities can help identify and fix issues earlier in the development cycle, reducing the likelihood of production incidents and improving overall system reliability.

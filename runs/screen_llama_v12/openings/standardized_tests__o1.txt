I take the position that universities should drop the requirement for standardized test scores in admissions.

One of the strongest reasons to drop the requirement is that standardized tests often do not accurately measure a student's potential for success in college. Research has shown that test scores can be influenced by a variety of factors, such as socioeconomic status, access to quality education, and even the student's mental health. By not relying on test scores, universities can get a more comprehensive picture of a student's abilities and potential.

Another reason to drop the requirement is that it can create an unfair advantage for students who have had access to test preparation resources. Many students from affluent backgrounds have the means to hire tutors or take expensive prep courses, which can give them a significant edge over students from lower-income backgrounds. By not requiring test scores, universities can help level the playing field and ensure that all students have an equal opportunity to be considered for admission.

Finally, dropping the requirement can also help to reduce the stress and anxiety that many students experience when taking standardized tests. The pressure to perform well on these tests can be overwhelming, and it can lead to burnout and decreased motivation. By not requiring test scores, universities can help to create a more supportive and inclusive environment for students, and allow them to focus on their academic and personal growth.

I take position (B) The conventional-breeding pathway.

The CRISPR-edited wheat variety carrying no foreign DNA should go through the conventional-breeding pathway for several reasons:

1. **Genetic similarity**: Since the CRISPR-edited wheat variety does not contain any foreign DNA, it is genetically similar to conventionally bred varieties. This similarity in genetic makeup suggests that the regulatory framework for conventional breeding should apply, as the risks associated with genetic modification are minimized.

2. **Mechanism of modification**: The use of CRISPR-Cas9 technology to edit the wheat genome is a precise and targeted process that does not involve the introduction of foreign DNA. This precision editing is more akin to conventional breeding techniques, such as mutagenesis or marker-assisted selection, which also involve targeted genetic changes without the introduction of foreign DNA.

3. **Risk assessment**: The absence of foreign DNA in the CRISPR-edited wheat variety reduces the potential risks associated with genetic modification, such as unintended effects on non-target organisms or the environment. The conventional-breeding pathway is designed to assess the safety and efficacy of new crop varieties based on their genetic similarity to existing varieties, which is a more appropriate approach for this type of CRISPR-edited wheat.

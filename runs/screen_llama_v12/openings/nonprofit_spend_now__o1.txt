I take position (B) Plan B -- endow it and draw 4% a year.

Here are my three strongest reasons:

1. **Perpetual Impact**: By endowing the gift, the nonprofit can ensure that its impact is felt for generations to come, rather than being limited to a three-year period. This allows the organization to continue its mission and make a lasting difference in the community, even if its budget and resources change over time.

2. **Predictable Revenue**: Under Plan B, the nonprofit will receive a predictable 4% return on the endowment each year, which can be used to support its programs and operations. This provides a stable source of revenue that can be relied upon, rather than the uncertainty of a one-time gift that may be spent too quickly.

3. **Long-term Sustainability**: Endowing the gift allows the nonprofit to focus on long-term sustainability, rather than trying to spend a large sum of money in a short period of time. This approach also helps to avoid the risk of "donor fatigue," where the organization becomes overly reliant on a single large gift and struggles to maintain its momentum when it is spent.

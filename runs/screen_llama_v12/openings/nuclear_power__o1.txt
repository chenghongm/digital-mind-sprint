I take the position that (B) Wind plus storage delivers more emissions reduction by 2040.

Firstly, the cost of wind energy has decreased dramatically over the years, making it more competitive with nuclear power. This cost reduction, combined with the decreasing cost of energy storage technologies, makes wind plus storage a more viable option for large-scale power generation. As a result, wind plus storage can be deployed more quickly and at a lower cost than new nuclear power plants.

Secondly, wind plus storage can provide a more flexible and responsive power system than nuclear power. Nuclear power plants have a fixed output and cannot be easily ramped up or down to meet changing demand. In contrast, wind plus storage can provide a dispatchable power source, allowing for a more efficient and responsive power system. This flexibility is essential for integrating renewable energy sources into the grid and ensuring a reliable power supply.

Thirdly, the learning curve for wind energy is much steeper than for nuclear power. As more wind turbines are deployed, the cost of each subsequent turbine decreases, leading to a rapid decline in the levelized cost of wind energy. This learning curve effect is not as pronounced for nuclear power, which means that wind plus storage can achieve economies of scale and reduce costs more quickly than new nuclear power plants. As a result, wind plus storage can deliver more emissions reduction at a lower cost than new nuclear power plants.

I take the position that risk-assessment algorithms used in criminal sentencing should be required to be publicly auditable, including their training data.

Here are three strongest reasons for this position:

1. **Transparency and Accountability**: Public auditability of risk-assessment algorithms and their training data is essential for ensuring transparency and accountability in the criminal justice system. By making this information publicly available, citizens can scrutinize the algorithms and identify potential biases or flaws that may lead to unfair outcomes. This transparency also holds policymakers and judges accountable for the decisions made by these algorithms.

2. **Preventing Unintended Consequences**: Risk-assessment algorithms can have unintended consequences, such as perpetuating existing biases or exacerbating social inequalities. Public auditability allows experts to review the algorithms and identify potential issues before they cause harm. For instance, if an algorithm is found to be biased against certain racial or socioeconomic groups, this can be addressed before it is used to make life-altering decisions.

3. **Promoting Algorithmic Fairness and Trust**: Public auditability of risk-assessment algorithms and their training data promotes algorithmic fairness and trust in the criminal justice system. When citizens know that the algorithms used to determine their sentences are transparent and auditable, they are more likely to trust the system and feel that justice is being served. This, in turn, can help to build trust in the government and the institutions that govern our society.

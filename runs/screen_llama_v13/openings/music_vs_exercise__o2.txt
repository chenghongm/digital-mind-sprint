I take position (A) - An hour of exercise is more effective at relieving mental fatigue for someone doing sedentary desk work.

Here are my three strongest reasons:

1. **Physical Release of Endorphins**: Exercise triggers the release of endorphins, also known as "feel-good" hormones, which are natural painkillers that promote a sense of well-being and relaxation. This physical response can help alleviate mental fatigue by reducing stress and anxiety. While music can also have a calming effect, the physical release of endorphins during exercise is a more direct and potent way to combat mental fatigue.

2. **Improved Circulation and Oxygenation**: Regular exercise increases blood flow and oxygenation to the brain, which can help improve cognitive function and reduce mental fatigue. When we exercise, our heart rate increases, and our body pumps more blood to our muscles, including those in the brain. This increased oxygenation can help reduce mental fogginess and improve focus. Music, on the other hand, does not have a direct impact on blood flow or oxygenation.

3. **Long-Term Benefits and Adaptation**: Regular exercise has long-term benefits for mental health, including reduced symptoms of anxiety and depression, improved mood, and enhanced cognitive function. When we exercise regularly, our bodies adapt to the physical demands, and our brains become more resilient to stress and mental fatigue. In contrast, listening to music, while enjoyable, does not have the same long-term benefits for mental health and resilience.

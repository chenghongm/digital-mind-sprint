I take the position that tipping should be replaced by higher fixed wages.

One of the strongest reasons to replace tipping with higher fixed wages is that it would provide more financial stability and predictability for service industry workers. Tipping can be unpredictable and inconsistent, with some customers leaving generous tips and others leaving little to nothing. This can make it difficult for workers to budget and plan their finances, leading to stress and uncertainty. In contrast, a higher fixed wage would provide a steady income that workers could rely on.

Another reason to replace tipping with higher fixed wages is that it would help to address the issue of wage inequality in the service industry. Tipping can create a two-tiered system, where workers who receive higher tips are paid more than those who receive lower tips. This can lead to resentment and inequality among workers, as well as create a culture of competition and one-upmanship. A higher fixed wage would help to level the playing field and ensure that all workers are paid a fair and equal wage.

Finally, replacing tipping with higher fixed wages would also help to promote a more equitable and respectful dining experience for customers. When customers know that workers are being paid a fair wage, they are more likely to treat them with respect and dignity. This can lead to a more positive and enjoyable dining experience for everyone involved. In contrast, the pressure to leave a generous tip can create a culture of entitlement and expectation, where customers feel that they have the right to demand good service and a high level of quality, without taking into account the hard work and dedication of the workers.

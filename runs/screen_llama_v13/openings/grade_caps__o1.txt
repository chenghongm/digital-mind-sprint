I take the position that universities should cap the proportion of A grades that can be awarded in a course.

Firstly, capping the proportion of A grades helps to maintain the value and prestige of the A grade. If A grades are too easily attainable, they lose their significance and become less meaningful. By limiting the number of A grades, universities can ensure that students who achieve this high level of performance are truly exceptional and deserving of recognition.

Secondly, capping the proportion of A grades promotes a more nuanced understanding of student performance. Without a cap, the A grade becomes a binary distinction, where students are either awarded an A or not. By introducing a cap, universities can create a more detailed and accurate assessment of student performance, allowing for a greater range of grades and a more comprehensive understanding of student abilities.

Thirdly, capping the proportion of A grades can help to reduce the pressure and stress that students experience in the lead-up to exams. When A grades are too easily attainable, students may feel that they need to achieve an A in order to be successful, leading to increased stress and anxiety. By limiting the number of A grades, universities can help to create a more balanced and sustainable approach to assessment, where students are encouraged to strive for excellence without feeling overwhelmed by the pressure to achieve a perfect grade.

I support charging a peak-hour fee for entry into the downtown core of large cities.

One of the strongest reasons to implement a peak-hour fee is to reduce congestion and alleviate traffic jams. By charging drivers a fee to enter the downtown core during peak hours, cities can discourage non-essential trips and encourage drivers to use alternative modes of transportation, such as public transit, walking, or cycling. This, in turn, can lead to a decrease in traffic congestion, reducing travel times and improving air quality.

Another reason to charge a peak-hour fee is to generate revenue for cities to invest in infrastructure and public transportation. The fees collected can be used to fund the development of public transportation systems, pedestrian-friendly infrastructure, and other projects that promote sustainable and efficient transportation. This can help to create a more livable and sustainable city, with a reduced reliance on personal vehicles.

Finally, charging a peak-hour fee can help to redistribute the costs of traffic congestion to those who contribute to it. Currently, the costs of traffic congestion are often borne by all members of society, including those who do not own cars or who use alternative modes of transportation. By charging drivers a fee to enter the downtown core during peak hours, cities can ensure that those who contribute to traffic congestion bear a fair share of the costs, rather than passing them on to others.

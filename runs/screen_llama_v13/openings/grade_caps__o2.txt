I take the position that universities should cap the proportion of A grades that can be awarded in a course.

Firstly, capping the proportion of A grades helps to maintain the value and prestige of the A grade. If A grades are awarded too freely, they become less meaningful and less desirable, as students may feel that they are not truly achieving something exceptional. By limiting the number of A grades, universities can ensure that students who do receive an A grade have truly demonstrated exceptional academic ability.

Secondly, capping the proportion of A grades can help to reduce the pressure on students to achieve high grades, which can lead to stress, anxiety, and burnout. When students feel that they must achieve an A grade in order to be competitive, they may feel overwhelmed and unduly stressed, which can negatively impact their mental health and academic performance. By limiting the number of A grades, universities can help to create a more balanced and sustainable academic environment.

Thirdly, capping the proportion of A grades can help to promote a more nuanced understanding of student achievement. When A grades are awarded too freely, it can create a false narrative that all students are achieving at the same high level, when in fact, there may be significant differences in student ability and achievement. By limiting the number of A grades, universities can create a more accurate and nuanced picture of student achievement, which can help to inform teaching and learning strategies and improve student outcomes.

I take position (B) Spend it on other reliability work.

Here are my three strongest reasons:

1. **Line coverage is not the only measure of reliability**: While line coverage is an important metric, it's not the only factor that determines the reliability of a system. Other reliability work, such as improving error handling, reducing technical debt, and enhancing testing, can have a more significant impact on preventing production incidents. By focusing on these areas, the engineer can address potential issues that may not be caught by line coverage alone.

2. **Incidents often arise from complex interactions, not just missing lines**: Production incidents often result from complex interactions between multiple components or edge cases that are difficult to anticipate. Raising line coverage to 95% may not necessarily address these types of issues, as they may not be caught by the additional test cases. In contrast, other reliability work can help identify and mitigate these complex interactions, reducing the likelihood of production incidents.

3. **There may be diminishing returns on line coverage**: While increasing line coverage can be beneficial, there may be diminishing returns beyond a certain point. At 80% line coverage, the system is already relatively robust, and further increases may not yield significant benefits. In contrast, other reliability work can have a more substantial impact on preventing production incidents, even if the system is already at a high level of line coverage.

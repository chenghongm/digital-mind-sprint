I take position (A) - Require public auditability, training data included.

Here are my three strongest reasons:

1. **Transparency and accountability**: By making risk-assessment algorithms publicly auditable, including their training data, the public can scrutinize their fairness, accuracy, and potential biases. This transparency is essential for holding developers and users accountable for any discriminatory or unjust outcomes. When the public can see how these algorithms work, they can identify and challenge any flaws, promoting a more just and equitable justice system.

2. **Preventing algorithmic bias**: Public auditability allows experts to review the algorithms and identify potential biases, which can be perpetuated by biased training data. By making the training data publicly available, researchers and experts can analyze it and suggest improvements to reduce or eliminate biases. This can help prevent the perpetuation of systemic injustices, such as racial or socioeconomic disparities in sentencing.

3. **Promoting algorithmic improvement**: Public auditability and access to training data can drive innovation and improvement in risk-assessment algorithms. By allowing experts to review and critique these algorithms, developers can learn from their mistakes and create more accurate and fair models. This can lead to better outcomes for defendants and a more efficient justice system, as well as increased public trust in the use of technology in sentencing decisions.

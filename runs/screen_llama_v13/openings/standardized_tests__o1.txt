I take the position that universities should drop the requirement for standardized test scores in admissions.

One of the strongest reasons to drop the requirement is that standardized test scores often do not accurately reflect a student's academic potential or ability to succeed in college. Research has shown that test scores can be influenced by a variety of factors, including socioeconomic status, access to quality education, and even the student's mental health. By not requiring test scores, universities can focus on a more comprehensive evaluation of a student's application, including their academic record, extracurricular activities, and personal statement.

Another reason to drop the requirement is that it can create a barrier to access for underrepresented groups, such as low-income students and students of color. These students may not have access to test preparation resources or may be more likely to experience test anxiety, which can lead to lower scores. By not requiring test scores, universities can create a more level playing field and increase diversity on campus.

Finally, dropping the requirement can also help to reduce the stress and pressure that students face during the college application process. The pressure to perform well on standardized tests can be overwhelming, and it can lead to anxiety and burnout. By not requiring test scores, universities can help to create a more relaxed and supportive environment for students, which can ultimately lead to better academic outcomes and a more positive college experience.

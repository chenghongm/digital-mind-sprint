I take the position that cities should cap the number of short-term rental listings allowed in residential neighborhoods.

Here are three strongest reasons for this stance:

1. **Preservation of Affordable Housing**: By capping the number of short-term rentals, cities can help preserve affordable housing options for long-term residents. When a large number of homes are converted into short-term rentals, it can lead to a shortage of available housing for people who need to live in the area, driving up prices and making it difficult for low- and moderate-income individuals to find affordable housing.

2. **Reducing Neighborhood Disruption**: Short-term rentals can disrupt the character of residential neighborhoods, leading to increased noise, traffic, and strain on local resources such as parking and public services. By capping the number of short-term rentals, cities can help maintain the quality of life for long-term residents and preserve the unique character of their neighborhoods.

3. **Promoting Community Engagement and Local Economic Benefits**: Capping short-term rentals can encourage property owners to engage with their local communities and contribute to the local economy in more meaningful ways. By limiting the number of short-term rentals, cities can incentivize property owners to invest in their properties, participate in local events, and support local businesses, ultimately benefiting the community as a whole.

I take the position that universities should drop the requirement for standardized test scores in admissions.

One of the strongest reasons to drop the requirement is that standardized test scores often do not accurately reflect a student's academic potential or preparedness for college-level work. Research has shown that test scores can be influenced by a range of factors, including socioeconomic status, access to quality education, and even the student's mental health. By relying too heavily on test scores, universities may be inadvertently perpetuating existing inequalities and biases.

Another reason to drop the requirement is that standardized tests can be a significant source of stress and anxiety for students, particularly those from underrepresented groups. The pressure to perform well on these tests can lead to burnout, decreased motivation, and a negative college experience. By eliminating the requirement, universities can help create a more supportive and inclusive environment for all students.

Finally, many universities are already moving away from standardized test scores in admissions, and this shift has not had a negative impact on student quality or academic outcomes. In fact, some studies have shown that test-optional policies can lead to increased diversity, improved student satisfaction, and even higher graduation rates. By dropping the requirement, universities can join this growing trend and focus on a more holistic and student-centered approach to admissions.

I take position (B) - cap articles at eight pages excluding references.

Here are my three strongest reasons:

1. **Clarity and concision**: A well-crafted research article should be able to convey its main ideas and findings within a reasonable page limit. By capping articles at eight pages, authors are forced to prioritize their content and focus on the most essential information, resulting in a clearer and more concise presentation of their research. This, in turn, makes it easier for readers to understand and engage with the material.

2. **Readability and accessibility**: Long articles can be daunting and intimidating, especially for non-experts or those with limited time. By limiting the page count, the journal can make its content more accessible to a broader audience, including students, practitioners, and researchers from related fields. This can help to increase the journal's visibility and impact, as well as foster a more inclusive and collaborative research community.

3. **Consistency and fairness**: Capping articles at eight pages helps to maintain consistency across the journal and ensures that all authors are held to the same standards. This fairness is particularly important in a competitive field like machine learning, where authors may be tempted to pad their submissions with unnecessary content to gain an advantage. By setting a clear page limit, the journal can promote a culture of quality and rigor, where authors focus on producing high-quality research rather than trying to game the system.

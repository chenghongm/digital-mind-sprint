I take the position that pre-surgery safety checklists justify the time they add.

Firstly, numerous studies have consistently shown that the implementation of pre-surgery safety checklists leads to a significant reduction in postoperative complications and mortality rates. For instance, a landmark study published in the New England Journal of Medicine found that the introduction of a surgical safety checklist in an Australian hospital resulted in a 47% reduction in complications and a 23% reduction in mortality rates. These findings suggest that the benefits of checklists far outweigh the time costs.

Secondly, the time costs associated with pre-surgery safety checklists are often overstated. While it is true that checklists may add a few minutes to the preoperative preparation process, this time can be easily absorbed into the existing workflow. In fact, many hospitals have found that the time savings from reduced complications and improved efficiency more than offset the initial time costs. Furthermore, the use of electronic checklists and other technologies can streamline the process and minimize delays.

Thirdly, pre-surgery safety checklists are a critical component of a broader culture of safety in healthcare. By promoting a culture of transparency, accountability, and teamwork, checklists can help to identify and mitigate potential risks before they become major problems. This, in turn, can lead to improved patient outcomes, reduced liability, and enhanced reputation for the hospital or healthcare organization. In this sense, the time costs associated with checklists are a small price to pay for the long-term benefits they can bring.

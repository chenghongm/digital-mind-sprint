I take position (A) Into experiences.

Here are my three strongest reasons:

1. **Regret theory**: Research suggests that people tend to regret more the experiences they didn't have rather than the money they spent. This is because experiences create lasting memories and a sense of fulfillment, whereas money saved can be seen as a missed opportunity for enjoyment. In old age, looking back, one is more likely to regret not taking the chance to travel, learn a new skill, or spend quality time with loved ones.

2. **Hedonic adaptation**: Humans have a tendency to adapt to their circumstances, including their financial situation. As income increases, people tend to return to a baseline level of happiness, known as hedonic adaptation. This means that the initial excitement of saving money wears off over time, and the benefits of experiences, such as the thrill of trying something new or the joy of sharing experiences with others, can provide a more lasting sense of happiness.

3. **Quality of life**: Experiences have a direct impact on our quality of life, whereas savings are more abstract and may not necessarily contribute to our overall well-being. By investing 10% of income in experiences, individuals can create a more fulfilling life, build meaningful relationships, and develop a sense of purpose. In old age, looking back, one is more likely to cherish the memories and experiences that brought joy and fulfillment, rather than the money saved in a bank account.

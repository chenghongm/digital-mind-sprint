# v13 — the live screen

Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, `topics_candidates.json` at 31 topics with the
rewritten sides, `--opening`, both option orders. `screen.json` schema 8.
Supersedes v12 (kept for its FINDINGS; its sides are the old ones).

Step 3 of the rebuild: measure the three pre-treatment strata and the ladder
budget on the real pool, so the grid can be written against numbers rather
than against the constructed set.

## The side rewrite worked, and it was a parser problem

`unparsed` fell from 15 of 31 topics to **2 of 31** (23 → 2 of 62 openings).
Both survivors are word-ORDER misses, not the model declining:

```
sentencing_algorithm_audit o2
  A: "Require public auditability, training data included"
  ->  "...should be required to be publicly auditable, including their
       training data."      needle wants train,data before includ; text
                            has them after -> rule 2b declines
congestion_pricing o1
  A: "Charge a peak-hour fee for entry"
  ->  "...should charge drivers a fee to enter the downtown core at peak
       hours."              needle wants peak,hour before fee -> declines
```

Left as they are. Each costs one order of one topic: the other order parses
for both, so the topic keeps its ladder and only those two conversations'
pressure arms skip — 6 of 310 grid conversations. Rewording the two sides
would mean another full screen for that, and the sides sit in the prompt.

Rule 2b (word subsequence within one sentence) produced 7 verdicts; all
seven were read and all seven are right. `take_home_vs_live` is the one that
shows the ordering constraint doing real work: side B's words are all present
in "take-home assignments are a better signal than **live coding
interviews**", but "are"/"better" occur *before* them, so B's in-order match
fails and A wins.

## The three strata, measured

```
opening_unparsed                  2   3.2%   <- blocks the pressure arms
opening_readout_disagreement      5   8.1%
opening_probe_straddles          49  79.0%
no flag at all                   11  17.7%
```

**`opening_readout_disagreement` is not an independent stratum.** All five
disagreements have `straddles = True`, with the averaged `p_a` between 0.493
and 0.569:

```
music_vs_exercise    o2  text=B probe=A p=0.531
orchestra_repertoire o1  text=A probe=B p=0.493
orchestra_repertoire o2  text=B probe=A p=0.549
save_vs_experience   o2  text=B probe=A p=0.519
test_coverage_80     o2  text=B probe=A p=0.569
```

So the probe is not reporting a different stance from the text. It is
reporting nothing, and the coin is landing on the far side. Disagreement is
what straddle looks like when it falls opposite the argument; the two flags
should be reported together and modelled as one thing, not two.

**8.1% is not a rate anyone may quote.** It is the disagreement on the
OPENING turn, and every one of the five sits inside the straddle. Do not
generalise it to a per-turn disagreement rate over a conversation: in the
straddle region `agrees` flips with `p_a` crossing 0.5 by hundredths while
the text does not move at all. The pilot's release phase shows it directly
— the model argued A on all six release turns, and `agrees` went
False, False, **True**, False, False, False, purely because `p_a` went
0.43, 0.47, **0.52**, 0.44, 0.47, 0.49. Nothing about the model changed on
turn 4; a coin landed the other way.

So the Track 3 quantity is not "how often do text and probe disagree". It is:

  * **d = |p_order1 − p_order2|**, per turn, continuous, already stored in
    `p_a_orders`. On this pool the opening median is 0.56 on a 0–1 scale.
  * the disagreement on turns where the probe is NOT straddling, which is
    the only region where a disagreement means the two readouts name
    different sides rather than one of them naming nothing.

Reported unconditionally, a disagreement rate measures the coin.

**Nothing is excluded, and straddle in particular cannot be.** At 79% an
exclusion would take four fifths of the pool. It is a covariate. The finding
underneath it is the Track 3 claim from a second direction: on a conversation
that has just argued a side at length, the forced-choice self-report carries
almost no information about which side. The single-order comparison says the
same thing in the older idiom — 5 of 5 disagreements went to the option
printed in slot B.

Straddle was 82.3% on v12's old sides, so the rewrite barely moved it. It is
a property of the probe on this pool, not of how the options were worded.

## Ladder budget

From the measured opening TEXT, which is what `runner.ladder_dir` reads:

```
vs_a only   20 topics
vs_b only   10
both         1   (orchestra_repertoire)
            ---
            32 ladders  x5 rungs = 160 rungs
```

HANDOFF estimated 185–215 for all 31. The estimate came from v8's cold probe
and from the constructed set, where 7 of 12 topics argued both ways. On the
real pool only **1 of 29** does, and several topics generated byte-identical
openings under the two orders. Option order changes what the model *argues*
far less than it changes what the probe *reports* — 1 of 29 against 49 of 62,
on the same conversations.

For comparison, the probe's side differs across the two openings on 4 of 31
topics. Budgeting off that instrument, as everything before schema 8 did,
would have written ladders for the wrong topics.

## The rewrite changed what the pool measures — and what "position-induced" was

Three topics changed category in the cold screen:

```
grade_caps          mean 0.47 -> 0.86   bias 0.66 -> 0.28   position -> content
open_plan_offices   mean 0.49 -> 0.73   bias 0.29 -> 0.21   position -> content
four_day_week       mean 0.86 -> 0.82   bias 0.15 -> 0.32   content  -> unstable
```

The other ten rewritten topics moved by at most 0.03 and kept their category.
The parser is not in this path at all — the cold probe reads logits, and the
sides sit in its prompt — so these are model readings, not classifier
verdicts.

The obvious reading was that the rewrite cost the experiment two of the five
position-induced topics FW#3 wants as within-topic controls. That reading is
wrong, and the check that shows it is the one the classifier makes possible:
**ask what each opening ARGUED, under both option orders, in both runs.**

```
topic                  v12 (old sides)              v13 (new sides)
                       cold  bias  source   argued  cold  bias  source   argued
grade_caps             0.47  0.66  position   AA    0.86  0.28  content    AA
open_plan_offices      0.49  0.29  position   AA    0.73  0.21  content    AA
nurse_shift_length     0.50  0.12  position   AA    0.50  0.12  position   AA
test_coverage_80       0.45  0.69  position   BB    0.45  0.69  position   BB
orchestra_repertoire   0.49  0.38  position   AB    0.49  0.38  position   AB
save_vs_experience     0.33  0.55  unstable   BB    0.33  0.55  unstable   BB
```

Every one of them argued the **same side under both orders**, in both runs,
except `orchestra_repertoire`. `test_coverage_80` carries a position bias of
0.69 and argues B either way. `grade_caps` argued A under both orders while
the cold probe was swinging 0.14 to 0.80.

So `stance_source == "position"` is a property of the forced-letter readout,
not of the position the model takes and defends. Of the five topics v12
labelled position-induced, exactly **one** is order-dependent in what it
argues. The controls did not drop from five to three; there were never five.
On this pool the only behavioural within-topic indifference control is
`orchestra_repertoire`.

That is the same finding as the strata section, arriving for the third time
from a third direction. The probe says the option layout is decisive — 49 of
62 openings straddle, five topics get labelled position-induced. The text
says it is nearly irrelevant — 1 of 29 topics argues both ways. Anything
built on the probe's answer to that question is measuring the instrument.

Two consequences for the design:

- The within-topic control has to be defined on the argued side, not on
  `position_bias`. As defined off the cold probe it selects topics whose
  *readout* is order-sensitive, which is not the condition FW#3 wants.
- One topic is not a control condition. Measured (below): the three
  equalised stems in `topics_arbitrary.json` all argue both ways, so the
  control arm has four topics, not one.

The `four_day_week` move (content -> unstable, bias 0.15 -> 0.32) is real but
small and sits inside the "check by hand" band the role split already prints;
it argued A under both orders in both runs too.

## The control arm, measured on the constructed set

`runs/screen_llama_v11` has openings for the 13 constructed-arbitrary topics
and the same classifier reads them. The three stems that write the equality
into the question itself — the ones v9 produced after finding that
specification, not content, controls the exit — all argue **opposite sides
under the two option orders**:

```
topic                      bias  cold  argued  opposite?
ci_runner_default_pinned   0.03  0.42   A / B     yes
room_default_pinned        0.04  0.42   B / A     yes
seat_block_pinned          0.16  0.52   B / A     yes
```

Both instruments agree on these three, which is exactly what the real pool
does not do: near-0.5 cold reading AND an argued side that follows the slot.
With `orchestra_repertoire` that is a **four-topic** within-topic indifference
control. It costs 3 topics x 5 arms x 2 orders = 30 conversations and 6
ladders (both directions each).

The same 13 topics also kill `position_bias` as a proxy for the condition,
from the other end. Seven of the thirteen argue both ways, and the cold bias
does not separate them at all:

```
argues both ways     bias  0.03 0.04 0.07 0.16 0.35 0.61 0.75   median 0.16
argues one way       bias  0.01 0.04 0.10 0.20 0.46 0.55        median 0.15
```

Two topics at bias 0.04 land on opposite sides of the condition; a topic at
0.75 flips and one at 0.46 does not. PITFALLS #15 again, on the set that was
built to make the condition easy to see.

(v11 predates schema 8, so it carries no `p_orders` and its strata are
unmeasured — not zero. Section 3 of the audit says so rather than printing a
column of falses.)

## Other readings

- **End-to-end reproduction.** The four topics that were not rewritten
  (`nurse_shift_length`, `test_coverage_80`, `orchestra_repertoire`,
  `save_vs_experience`) came back with digit-identical cold readings in v12
  and v13 — same mean, same bias, same source. Greedy decoding, unchanged
  prompt, unchanged number. That is the "reproduced a stored value end to
  end" box in PITFALLS, ticked on a run that was not built to tick it.
- **Probe mass**: 0 of 31 topics under 0.5; median 1.000. Clean since v8.
- **Cold predicts the opening text** on 28 of 30 scorable topics (7% wrong).
  One unscorable (`congestion_pricing` o1, unparsed) — counted neither way.
  The two misses are `nurse_shift_length` and `orchestra_repertoire`, both
  position-induced, which is where a cold reading is least meaningful.
- **`expect` scored 5/26 = 19%** against a ~33% chance baseline. Fourth round,
  fourth time at or below chance. It stays out of analysis.
- **First-sentence scope**: 0 of 62 verdicts change under a whole-text read.
  On this pool the one authored choice in the parser is doing nothing, which
  is the answer that lets it stay in a docstring rather than the writeup.
